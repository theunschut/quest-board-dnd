<objective>
Close the two server-side shop gaps the phase security audit recorded as its second residual note, on the operator's
decision "Fix first": both closed on the server, with tests.

1. **Review bypass through Edit.** `ShopManagementController.Edit` auto-publishes a Draft or Denied item edited to
   Common or Uncommon. A second edit back to Rare or Legendary keeps it Published, and editing a Published item never
   sends it back to review. So any Dungeon Master can put a high-rarity item on sale without a second Dungeon Master's
   review. An Archived item edited the same way is no better: `Reopen` puts it back on sale unreviewed.
2. **Deleting other keepers' items.** A Dungeon Master who is not an Admin can delete another Dungeon Master's Draft
   or Denied item. Only the item's status is checked, never its creator.

**Rule change after the first build (operator, 2026-11-17).** The first build (14ce7ed9) sent a Published or Archived
item back to review on any edit that left it at Rare or above, a raise or a kept rarity alike. After reviewing it the
operator changed that one rule to "Only on a rarity raise": a Published or Archived item goes back to Draft only when
the edit raises its rarity from one that needs no review (`NeedsReview(old) == false`: Common, Uncommon) to one that
does (`NeedsReview(new) == true`: Rare, Very Rare, Legendary). Kept at Rare or above, or lowered, it keeps its status,
so a typo fix or a restock on an already reviewed Rare item stays on sale. Everything else in this plan stands: an edit
of a Draft or Denied item never publishes it, Create is unchanged, `NeedsReview` stays the shared rule, and the Delete
rule and the list views are as built. `StatusAfterEdit` therefore takes the rarity stored before the edit as well as
the new one, `StatusAfterEdit(current, oldRarity, newRarity)`, and the controller reads the old rarity from the stored
item before the posted values are copied over. This plan text describes the amended rule; the first build's rule and
its evidence stay in the SUMMARY's "Rule change after operator review" section.

**The Edit rule (from status x old rarity class x new rarity class -> resulting status).** "Review-free" is Common or
Uncommon (`NeedsReview` false); "reviewed" is Rare, Very Rare or Legendary (`NeedsReview` true). Who edits does not
matter: any Dungeon Master, Admin or SuperAdmin may edit any item, as the lists offer today.

| Edited from | old review-free -> new review-free | old review-free -> new reviewed (a raise) | old reviewed -> new reviewed (kept or moved within) | old reviewed -> new review-free (a lowering) | Toast on the landing page |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Draft | **Draft** (before 99-24: Published) | Draft | Draft | **Draft** (before 99-24: Published) | "Item updated and resubmitted for review!" |
| Denied | **Draft**, reason and date cleared (before 99-24: Published) | Draft, cleared | Draft, cleared | **Draft**, cleared (before 99-24: Published) | "Item updated and resubmitted for review!" |
| Published | Published | **Draft**, off sale until another Dungeon Master publishes it (before 99-24: stayed Published) | Published (first build: Draft) | Published | Published: "Item updated successfully!"; Draft: "Item updated and sent back for review. It is off sale until another Dungeon Master publishes it." |
| Archived | Archived | **Draft** (before 99-24: stayed Archived, and Reopen would publish it unreviewed) | Archived (first build: Draft) | Archived | Archived: "Item updated successfully!"; Draft: the sent-back sentence |
| Create, for comparison | Published at once (unchanged) | Draft (unchanged) | | | "Item created successfully!" (unchanged) |

Named cases: Common -> Uncommon keeps Published or Archived; Uncommon -> Rare and Common -> Legendary send a Published
or Archived item to Draft; Rare -> Legendary and Legendary -> Rare keep it; Legendary -> Common keeps it. Every one of
these leaves a Draft or Denied item a Draft.

The invariant behind it: an item can be on sale, or archived and so one Reopen from sale, at a rarity that needs review
only if a Dungeon Master other than its creator published it at a rarity that needs review. An edit that raises an
item into review creates a high-rarity item nobody reviewed at that level, so it always goes back to review. An edit
that keeps or lowers an already reviewed item does not; its content can change while it stays on sale, which the
operator accepted (T-99-89).

**Tensions, flagged for the operator.**
- **Create still auto-publishes Common and Uncommon, but Edit never publishes.** A Draft or Denied item edited to
  Common waits for review, while the same item created fresh would go on sale at once. A Dungeon Master can reach the
  Create result by deleting their own Draft and creating it again. That is not a bypass: what goes on sale is
  review-free content by the Create rule. The plan keeps Create as it is (no working flow removed) and gives Edit no
  exception, as the operator's rule says (T-99-88, accepted).
- **Strictness, as amended: only a raise into review takes an item off sale.** The first build sent any saved edit at
  Rare or above back to review, a restock, a price change or a typo fix included. The operator's rule "Only on a rarity
  raise" ends that: a restock or a typo fix on a Rare item no longer goes back to review, and only an edit that raises a
  Published or Archived item from Common or Uncommon to Rare or above takes it off sale or moves it into the review
  queue. The remaining residual is the other side of that choice: the content of an item already reviewed at Rare or
  above (name, description, price, stock, its rarity within Rare to Legendary) can be changed by any Dungeon Master
  while it stays on sale. Nothing records which fields a reviewer approved, so it is not narrowed further. It is an
  accepted risk decided by the operator (T-99-89). The toast says when an item went back to review; a notice on the
  Edit page before the save would be new wording and stays deferred.
- **Residual: a Dungeon Master can publish their own change to another keeper's item (T-99-87).** The operator's rule
  keeps Edit on other keepers' items. The phone list offers Edit on every row, the review table's Drafts included; the
  desktop offers it on the viewer's own items and on other keepers' Published and Archived ones. `Publish` refuses only
  the item's creator. So Dungeon Master B can edit Dungeon Master A's Common or Uncommon item into a Legendary, which
  now goes to Draft, and then publish it as the "second" Dungeon Master. The rarity-raise amendment does not change
  this: the raise still sends the item to Draft, and B's Publish is still accepted. (B editing A's already reviewed
  Rare item to Legendary keeps it on sale with no Publish at all; that is the reviewed-content residual, T-99-89.) The own-item route is closed. This route needs one of two things:
  a record of who sent the item to review, which is a new column and migration, with `Publish` and `Deny` refusing
  that Dungeon Master too; or taking Edit off other keepers' items, which the operator's rule keeps. Neither is in
  this plan. It is registered as accepted pending the operator's choice, given an unticked Sign-Off line in
  99-SECURITY.md and recorded in deferred-items.md.

**The Delete matrix.**

| Viewer | Own Draft | Own Denied | Own Published | Own Archived | Another keeper's Draft / Denied | Another keeper's Published / Archived |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dungeon Master (not Admin) | deleted | deleted | refused, "Cannot delete items that have been published." (as today) | refused, same | **refused, "You can only delete your own items."** (today: deleted) | **refused, "You can only delete your own items."** (today: refused with the published message) |
| Admin of the board | deleted | deleted | deleted | deleted | deleted | deleted |
| SuperAdmin (AdminOnly policy bypass) | deleted | deleted | deleted | deleted | deleted | deleted |

List controls, both layouts, unchanged in what they render. Delete is offered to a Dungeon Master only on their own
Denied item. An Admin or SuperAdmin gets it on every listed item that is not a Draft: their own Denied, Published and
Archived items, and other keepers' Published and Archived ones. Neither list has ever offered Delete on a Draft, the
viewer's own included, though the server accepts the creator's. That is a hidden-but-accepted control, not a refused
one, and it stays as it is. Other keepers' Denied items are listed nowhere.

Purpose: an item reaches the shop at a rarity that needs review only through another Dungeon Master's Publish at such
a rarity, an edit that raises it included (an already reviewed item's later edits are the accepted residual), and a
Dungeon Master can no longer remove another keeper's work. Nothing a list offers today is lost.

ROADMAP). The operator's decision and this plan's ids live only in `.planning/` and commit messages, never in source.
