Expand the tracer's single proven path along the axis the tracer deliberately left untouched: *why* a quest is in the feed at all. The tracer proved one route in — a confirmed player seat. This plan proves the second route in (running the session as Dungeon Master), proves the two routes cannot double-emit when one quest satisfies both, proves the routes that must stay closed (a waitlisted row), and proves the routes that must stay open regardless of label (a Spectator seat, an Assistant Dungeon Master seat, and a quest flagged as a Dungeon Master session).

Output: six behavioural facts on the live feed address, all in the file the tracer created.

Append three facts to the existing test class. Follow the tracer fact's shape exactly: clear the database, create users through the authentication helper, seed a one-shot board with an id distinct from the default seeded board and a name the fact controls, add memberships, seed quests and signups, mint a subscription through the real service, clear the active board, fetch with a plain client carrying no authorization, then assert on the body.

**Fact one — the Dungeon Master route.** The reader owns a finalized quest on a one-shot board they belong to, and holds **no** signup row on it at all. Seed no signup for the reader on that quest. Assert the quest's title line and its quest-namespaced identifier are both present in the body. This is the fact that would be missing if the query were rooted at signups alone: the person doing most of the board's scheduling would get the emptiest calendar.

**Fact two — the single-entry guarantee.** One finalized quest on a one-shot board where the reader is **both** the Dungeon Master **and** the holder of a confirmed seat. Seed both: the quest owned by the reader, and a confirmed signup for the reader on that same quest.

Assert three things, all of them counts rather than containments, because a duplicate is invisible to a containment assertion. The number of event openers in the whole body is exactly one. The number of occurrences of that quest's identifier value in the body is exactly one. The number of occurrences of its title line is exactly one.

Comment this fact with what it defends, in plain language and with no planning identifiers: the two conditions that put a quest in the feed can both be true of one quest, the identifier is derived from the quest alone, and two entries sharing one identifier is undefined behaviour on a reader's phone — so this fact fails the moment the query stops being rooted at quests and starts being two result sets merged after the fact.

**Fact three — the negative control.** On the same one-shot board the reader belongs to, seed a finalized quest owned by someone else with **no** signup row for the reader, alongside a second quest the reader is genuinely seated on. Assert the seated quest's title is present and the unrelated quest's title is not, and that the body carries exactly one event opener.

This fact matters more than it looks: without it, facts one and two are both satisfied by a query that returns every finalized quest on every one-shot board the reader belongs to, which is a real leak of the reader's own board's scheduling and is precisely the shape the locked "only quests the reader is signed up for" constraint rules out. Use two quests in one fetch rather than one quest in each of two fetches, so a query that collapsed to board scope fails on the count assertion as well as the absence assertion.

Give every fact a name that states what it proves rather than which decision it came from — a reader of the suite in a year should learn the behaviour from the name, not need the planning directory.

**Fact one — a row is not a seat.** The reader holds a signup row on a finalized quest on a one-shot board they belong to, with the confirmed-seat flag **not** set: a waitlisted signup. Assert the quest's title and its identifier are both absent from the body, and that the body carries zero event openers.

Then, in the same fact, flip that same row's confirmed-seat flag through the seeding context and fetch again, asserting the quest now appears. Do it in one fact rather than two, because the pair is the behaviour: a promotion off the waitlist reaches the phone at the next fetch like any other change, and two separate facts would prove the two states without proving the transition between them. Note in a comment that this is the shape a real promotion takes — nothing else about the row changes.

Comment the accepted cost this fact locks in, in plain language: a waitlisted player gets no advance warning of a night they may well end up playing.

**Fact two — every seat kind counts.** In one fetch, seed three finalized quests on the same one-shot board, each with a confirmed signup row for the reader, one per seat kind: the default player kind, the spectator kind and the assistant Dungeon Master kind. Assert all three titles are present and the body carries exactly three event openers.

Use one fetch with three quests rather than three fetches, so a predicate that quietly filtered on seat kind fails on the count as well as on an absence. Read the seat-kind values from the enum rather than writing the integers as literals, so a future renumbering of the enum cannot leave the fact silently testing the wrong kind.

Comment why no seat-kind branch exists at all: the confirmed-seat flag is set unconditionally for the spectator and assistant kinds, and only a player ever lands on the waitlist, so everyone holding a confirmed seat is at the table that night whatever the seat is called.

**Fact three — the listing hide is not an access control.** A finalized quest on a one-shot board, flagged as a Dungeon Master session, owned by someone else, with a confirmed seat for the reader. Assert it appears.

Comment this one carefully and in plain language, because it looks like a bug to a reader who does not know the codebase: that flag hides a quest from the board listing, but the quest details request applies no gate whatsoever, so anyone holding the link can already open one. Honouring a seat that was actually granted therefore widens nothing. Record the accepted cost too: a Dungeon Master who flips that flag after players signed up leaves those readers' phones carrying a title the board no longer lists for them.

Do not write any fact asserting the flag hides a quest from the feed. That is the inverse of the locked decision and would need to be deleted by whoever discovers it.
